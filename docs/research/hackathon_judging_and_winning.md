# Environmental Hacks (Bharat Builds Tour, WeMakeDevs x AWS, Oct 8-11 2026): judging, requirements, and how to win

Research date: 2026-10-08 (day 1 of the event). Primary sources are the official WeMakeDevs event, rules, schedule and mentors pages and the organizer's own blog posts. Unless marked otherwise, everything refers to the 2026 edition. Items from First Commit (Event 01, Sept 2026), earlier WeMakeDevs hackathons, or other AWS events are labeled as such.

## Q1. What is the Bharat Builds Tour and Environmental Hacks: cities, dates, format, registration, tracks, prizes, themes?

### Takeaway
Environmental Hacks is stop 2 of a six-stop student hackathon tour run by WeMakeDevs with AWS Builder Center. It runs online across India from Thu Oct 8 to Sun Oct 11, 2026, with an optional in-person build day at DTU Delhi on Sat Oct 10. There are three tracks (Air; Heat and Water; Waste and Energy), each judged separately. Each track winner gets ₹2,00,000 cash plus $2,000 in AWS credits. Amazon fast-track interviews are offered on top of the prizes.

### Cited Findings
**Tour**
- The tour is a six-hackathon series run by WeMakeDevs with AWS Builder Center. Every stop is online and open to university students across India, and each has limited in-person seats in its host city. — [WeMakeDevs tour page](https://www.wemakedevs.org/aws)
- WeMakeDevs describes it as "six city hybrid hackathons" running September to December. — [WeMakeDevs on X](https://x.com/WeMakeDevs/status/2097179221428949423)
- Tour prizes are worth "₹1 crore" in gadgets and cloud credits. Posters for Events 01 and 02 advertise ₹20 lakh each. — [Tour page](https://www.wemakedevs.org/aws)
- Stops: 01 First Commit, Sept 17-20, 2026 (in person Sept 19, Bangalore). 02 Environmental Hacks, Oct 8-11, 2026 (in person Oct 10, Delhi). Stops 03-06 have no city announced yet. — [Tour page](https://www.wemakedevs.org/aws)
- Registration numbers: 5,000+ registered by early September, 10,000+ later, and "16,000+ participants" joined First Commit. — [X: 5,000+](https://x.com/WeMakeDevs/status/2096581007835029611); [X: 10,000+](https://x.com/WeMakeDevs/status/2099018267180356018); [X: 16,000+](https://x.com/WeMakeDevs/status/2103406969923219680)

## Q2. Official judging criteria, weights, submission requirements, and mandatory or bonus tech. Is "big data concepts" required?

### Takeaway
There are five published criteria, with no weights: Idea and Impact, Built on AWS (mandatory for prizes), Design and Usability, Execution, and Demo video. Judges see only the submission: a public repo, a demo video of up to 3 minutes, and a short writeup. There is no live demo, and anything not shown in the video does not count. No big-data, Spark, EMR, or Athena requirement appears anywhere in the official event page, rules, announcement, or organizer guides. The synopsis line "Big data concepts..." is not an event requirement.

### Cited Findings
**Criteria, in the order published, with no weights given**
1. "Idea and Impact": does it fix a real environmental problem, and what changes for the people affected? The announcement says most projects lose points on the second half, "what changes". — [Env Hacks page](https://www.wemakedevs.org/aws/environmental-hacks); [Announcement](https://www.wemakedevs.org/blogs/announcing-environmental-hacks)
2. "Built on AWS": the project uses AWS open-source tools or AWS services. This is mandatory for prizes. — [Env Hacks page](https://www.wemakedevs.org/aws/environmental-hacks)
3. "Design and usability": can someone outside the team understand and use it? — [Env Hacks page](https://www.wemakedevs.org/aws/environmental-hacks)
4. "Execution": does it work? "A working feature beats several unfinished ones." — [Env Hacks page](https://www.wemakedevs.org/aws/environmental-hacks)
5. "Demo video": three minutes, recorded, covering what it does, who it is for, and where AWS fits. There is no live demo. — [Env Hacks page](https://www.wemakedevs.org/aws/environmental-hacks)
- Projects built locally and projects deployed on AWS are scored equally. "Judging is based on what you built, not what you spent." — [Env Hacks page](https://www.wemakedevs.org/aws/environmental-hacks)
- **Discrepancy:** the organizer's planning and build guides list the five areas as idea and impact, built on AWS, "what you learned", execution, and demo. "What you learned" replaces design and usability in that list. The event page is the authoritative source, but a short "what we learned" line in the writeup covers both versions. — [How to plan a cloud project](https://www.wemakedevs.org/blogs/how-to-plan-a-cloud-project); [Real-data AI project guide](https://www.wemakedevs.org/blogs/how-to-build-a-real-data-ai-project-on-the-aws-open-source-stack)

**How judging works**
- "Judges score only the submission. There is no live demo or interview." Features that appear only in the writeup, or are missing from the video, do not count. — [Rules](https://www.wemakedevs.org/aws/rules)
- Venue and online projects are scored by the same panel using the same criteria. Decisions are final, and scores are not published. — [Rules](https://www.wemakedevs.org/aws/rules)

**Submission requirements (all mandatory)**
1. A public code repository.
2. A demo video of up to 3 minutes on YouTube, set to public or unlisted. Check that it opens in a signed-out browser.
3. A short writeup covering the problem, the build, and where AWS fits.
- Submit once per team through the event's form before the deadline. Late submissions are not accepted. — [Rules](https://www.wemakedevs.org/aws/rules)
- Blog prize: a write-up published on AWS Builder Center and linked in the submission. — [Env Hacks page](https://www.wemakedevs.org/aws/environmental-hacks)
- A deployed URL is not required, since local and deployed projects score equally. At First Commit (Event 01), a URL was required only for the "Ship It" track. — [Env Hacks page](https://www.wemakedevs.org/aws/environmental-hacks); [First Commit page](https://www.wemakedevs.org/aws/first-commit)
- No architecture diagram is mandated in the official pages found.

**Mandatory and allowed technology**
- "The project must use AWS, and the demo video must show it. Mentioning AWS only in the writeup is not enough." — [Rules](https://www.wemakedevs.org/aws/rules)
- Open-source tools that run locally with no AWS account ("Build It" examples): Strands Agents SDK, Finch, SAM CLI, LocalStack, OpenSearch, Cedar. — [Env Hacks page](https://www.wemakedevs.org/aws/environmental-hacks)
- Deployed services ("Ship It" examples): SageMaker AI, EKS, ECS, Lambda, API Gateway, Step Functions, EC2, App Runner, Amplify Hosting, S3, DynamoDB, RDS, Aurora, Cognito, CloudFront, Route 53, EventBridge, SQS, SNS, CloudWatch. "Other tools are allowed." — [Env Hacks page](https://www.wemakedevs.org/aws/environmental-hacks)
- AI coding tools are permitted, but "List every tool used in the writeup." — [Rules](https://www.wemakedevs.org/aws/rules)
- No page found requires or rewards Kiro, Amazon Q, or Bedrock specifically.

**Originality and disqualification**
- Building must happen between the event's opening and its deadline. Learning and planning beforehand is allowed. Projects started before the hackathon opened do not qualify "even if rewritten". — [Rules](https://www.wemakedevs.org/aws/rules)
- Grounds for disqualifying the whole team: copying someone else's work, presenting an old project as new, or "a repository history that does not match the event dates". — [Rules](https://www.wemakedevs.org/aws/rules)
- Third-party libraries, templates and public APIs are allowed if credited and properly licensed. — [Rules](https://www.wemakedevs.org/aws/rules)

**Big data**
- None of these pages mention big data, Spark, EMR, Athena, or data lakes as a requirement or criterion: the event page, the rules, the announcement, or the schedule. — [Env Hacks page](https://www.wemakedevs.org/aws/environmental-hacks); [Rules](https://www.wemakedevs.org/aws/rules); [Announcement](https://www.wemakedevs.org/blogs/announcing-environmental-hacks)
- The organizer's build guides target "weekend-scale" serverless or local projects and do not discuss Spark, EMR, Athena or big-data pipelines. — [Live-data serverless guide](https://www.wemakedevs.org/blogs/how-to-build-a-live-data-project-on-the-aws-serverless-stack); [Real-data AI guide](https://www.wemakedevs.org/blogs/how-to-build-a-real-data-ai-project-on-the-aws-open-source-stack)
- EMR Serverless and Athena are not on the organizers' example service lists, though "Other tools are allowed". — [Env Hacks page](https://www.wemakedevs.org/aws/environmental-hacks)

### Inferences
- "Big data concepts" is not an event requirement. The line most likely comes from another context, such as a college course or a project-review requirement; the team should confirm. For the hackathon, Spark on EMR Serverless and partitioned Parquet with Athena earn points only through "Built on AWS" and "Execution", and only if they are visible in the video and tied to user impact. A credible demo line would be: "the same Spark job that indexed Jodhpur re-runs for all of India".
- If the big-data layer risks the must-have flow (pin → history → fires → cited draft), cut it to a pre-computed artifact. One Spark run that writes the Jodhpur Parquet partition, shown for about 10 seconds in the video, is enough. The organizers' own guide says to "drop any service you can't tie to a visible part of the demo". — [How to plan a cloud project](https://www.wemakedevs.org/blogs/how-to-plan-a-cloud-project)
- OpenSearch and Strands are both on the organizers' named open-source list, so the lookalike search and the agent already satisfy "Built on AWS", even run locally. The Bedrock, Lambda and Amplify pieces add the deployed dimension.
- Repository-history risk: the team should create the repo after kickoff on Oct 8 and commit often from all three members' accounts. The synopsis PDF is planning, which is allowed, but no pre-written code should be imported.
- The video must visibly show AWS. Briefly show the console or the architecture with real resource names (Amplify app, Lambda, EMR Serverless job run, Bedrock model, OpenSearch index), not only a slide.
- Cost: "Judged on what you built, not what you spent", and credits are limited ($200 Free Tier plus a $25 top-up). Keep managed OpenSearch and EMR Serverless usage minimal (for example, run OpenSearch locally or on the smallest option) and tear resources down after recording. This is a judgment based on organizer guidance, not a pricing check.

### Gaps
- Judging weights are not published.
- Whether the writeup has a word limit or a required template is unknown, since the submission form was not visible.
- Whether multiple team members can each enter the blog prize is unclear: the prize goes to "5 people", but the blog is "linked in your submission".

## Q3. Who are the judges or mentors, and what have they said they value?

### Takeaway
No judges are named for Environmental Hacks. The mentors page lists 20 AWS employees, mostly cloud support, TAM, database and consultant roles, who are at DTU to "help you shape the idea, unblock the build". What the organizers value is best read from WeMakeDevs' own build guides: real, provable data; showing limits; a deterministic and verifiable core with the LLM constrained; and one end-to-end flow.

### Cited Findings
- The mentors page lists 20 AWS mentors and no judges. Among them: Arkodyuti Saha (Developer Experience Community Manager), Chanpreet Singh (Senior Lead Consultant, AI/ML), Shweta Chopra (Senior Database Engineer), Rishabh Kumar Dubey and Arpit Agarwal (AWS Professional Services), and several TAMs and Cloud Support Engineers. Its stated purpose is "help you shape the idea, unblock the build and get it over the line." — [Mentors](https://www.wemakedevs.org/aws/env/mentors)
- Delhi build-day speakers: Piyush Garg (Teachyst), Kunal Kushwaha (WeMakeDevs), Saiyam Pathak (Kubesimplify). They are not stated to be judges. — [Env Hacks page](https://www.wemakedevs.org/aws/environmental-hacks)
- Judges, organisers and Amazon jointly decide who gets fast-track interviews. — [Rules](https://www.wemakedevs.org/aws/rules)

**What the organizers' guides say they reward (stated for this tour)**
- Start with a specific user and one real example. Define the output the user receives, because "if you can't describe that output, you can't demo it." Reduce the project to one end-to-end flow. — [How to plan a cloud project](https://www.wemakedevs.org/blogs/how-to-plan-a-cloud-project)
- "Judges reward a single flow that works from start to finish over many partially built features." Freeze features before the final day. Being open about limitations is better than hiding them. — [How to plan a cloud project](https://www.wemakedevs.org/blogs/how-to-plan-a-cloud-project)
- Use real data and show where it stops. An interface that states what its data cannot detect is more credible than implying full coverage. Prefer a reproducible check (test suite or live smoke test) over an unverified claim. Prepare a slide on limits before anyone asks. — [Live-data serverless guide](https://www.wemakedevs.org/blogs/how-to-build-a-live-data-project-on-the-aws-serverless-stack)
- For AI projects: "put rules in data, not prompts". Make sources provable (download from official domains, SHA-256 hash, index with OpenSearch, and run a verification command that checks every quoted line appears in its source). Use the model only to explain a result that code has already computed, validate its output, and keep a deterministic fallback. Common mistakes include "claiming real data without a way to prove it" and "bundling static data and presenting it as live". — [Real-data AI guide](https://www.wemakedevs.org/blogs/how-to-build-a-real-data-ai-project-on-the-aws-open-source-stack)
- The announcement advises stating data sources and data freshness clearly in the demo, and writing one sentence describing the person you are building for. — [Announcement](https://www.wemakedevs.org/blogs/announcing-environmental-hacks)

### Inferences
- The organizers' "real-data AI project" guide describes almost exactly Saboot's rule-retrieval design: OpenSearch-indexed official documents, citations shown beside the claim, the model constrained. The team should lean into this. Show a verification step on screen (for example "12/12 cited clauses found verbatim in SWM Rules 2026 PDF, SHA-256 matched"), keep a template-based fallback draft, and avoid words like "violation confirmed". Use "possible violation of Rule X; verify with authority".
- With a mentor pool heavy in cloud support, database and TAM roles, mentors (and probably the judges, if drawn from similar pools) will likely value sound, cost-aware, well-explained architecture over flashy claims. A "Where AWS fits" table mapping each service to a visible function and a file is a cheap win. This is inference, since judge identities are unknown.

- No public statements from these specific AWS mentors about what they value were found.

## Q5. What general patterns win hackathons, and what are the common failure modes?

### Takeaway
Across judges and organizers, the consistent winners: meet every stated requirement first, fit all criteria (not just one), ship one finished flow on real data, tell a user story rather than list features, and be candid about limits. Common failures are missing basic requirements, polished presentation over thin code, backend with no usable UI, half-built features, and unclear team contribution. In a video-only contest like this one, the video is effectively the product.

### Cited Findings
**What judges look for (Devpost judges' panel)**
- Judges often check requirements first, and "many submissions miss basic ones". Projects should align with all judging criteria. Some entries "shoehorn" an unrelated idea. — [Devpost: judging tips](https://info.devpost.com/blog/hackathon-judging-tips)
- What stands out: a finished product someone would want to use, creativity beyond the starter template, understanding of the underlying problem, and openness about how it was built. — [Devpost](https://info.devpost.com/blog/hackathon-judging-tips)

## Q6. Recommended pitch and demo structure, and what judges should see in the first 30 seconds

### Takeaway
The organizers prescribe a 3-minute split: about 20 s on user and problem, 90 s on the working flow, 30 s on architecture, and the rest on one failure case and next steps. The video should be a screen recording of the real product, not slides. In the first 30 seconds a judge should know who the user is, what is broken today, and see the product producing the "proof".
