## Q1. Which citizen complaint apps exist in India and globally, and what are their resolution rates and known weaknesses?

### Takeaway
India already has many government complaint channels that cover garbage and burning: Swachhata (MoHUA), MCD 311 and Green Delhi, CPCB Sameer, Rajasthan Sampark/181 and CPGRAMS. All of them report high "resolved/disposed" rates, but field checks show closures that don't match the ground, and outside Delhi-NCR routing is weak (Sameer forwards by email to state boards). None of these apps, and none of the global apps (FixMyStreet, TrashOut, Litterati, OpenLitterMap), attaches satellite history, cites the applicable rule, or independently checks whether a "resolved" complaint was really fixed. That verification-and-escalation gap is where Saboot can stand out. "Another reporting app" is not.

### Cited Findings

**Swachhata (MoHUA)**
- A 2017 report put the app at about 11 lakh complaints in 10 months, with over 85% "resolved". Most complaints must be cleared within 12 hours (48 h for dead-animal removal), unresolved ones escalate, the agency uploads a photo as proof of resolution, and citizens can reopen — [India Environment Portal / TOI](https://www.indiaenvironmentportal.org.in/news-clippings/swachh-app-a-hit-10l-issues-sorted-out-in-10-months)
- The app locates complaints from the photo's geolocation. A separate Engineer app lets sanitary inspectors act on complaints — [Vikaspedia](https://vikaspedia.in/e-governance/mobile-governance/swachhata-app)
- The Quint tested the Swachh Delhi app at five sites. It received "resolved" reports, but the garbage was still there on revisit (Anand Vihar), and at Pragati Maidan nothing had changed on the same day the resolution came. This was a small sample, not a statistical study — [The Quint](https://www.thequint.com/news/india/swachh-delhi-app-works-but-in-virtual-world)
- In Jalandhar, residents reportedly uninstalled the app. 45 complaints were unresolved, some for a month, and 38 had been reopened (article undated) — [Tribune](https://m.tribuneindia.com/article/swachhata-app-loses-favour-with-jalandhar-residents/714798)
- A West Bengal pilot across 10 civic bodies resolved 48 of 72 complaints on day one, with rates as low as 33% and 40% in some municipalities (date unclear) — [UNI](https://test.uniindia.com/east/news/3863480.html)

**MCD 311 / Green Delhi**
- Green Delhi (2020) let citizens upload photo, video or audio of garbage burning with auto-location, routed complaints to departments with deadlines, required a photo on closure, and allowed reopening — [Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/delhi/kejriwal-launches-green-delhi-app-to-redress-pollution-complaints-4761)
- After the first months, about 2,300 complaints had been received and about 1,346 solved (about 58%). Most were about burning of garbage or plastic, and a 24x7 "Green War Room" with about 70 green marshals monitored them — [LatestLY quoting Gopal Rai](https://www.latestly.com/india/news/stubble-burning-delhi-govt-forms-panel-to-study-impact-of-pusa-bio-decomposer-solution-sprinkling-2131205.html/amp)
- MCD told the NGT that its 311 app received 7,28,341 complaints from July 2022 to October 2024, with 85.85% resolved. Yet of about 11,871 air-pollution complaints in October 2024, only 3,547 were resolved and 8,324 were pending — [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/planet/industry/rs-9535-lakh-fine-imposed-for-illegally-dumping-debris-in-delhi-mcd-report-to-ngt)
- MCD 311 handles garbage burning among other issues — [Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/delhi/delhi-mc-urges-residents-to-report-civic-issues-on-app)

**CPCB Sameer**
- Sameer 2.0 (September 2025) added a grievance channel. Citizens can photograph a burning garbage heap and file directly with CPCB — [Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/amp/story/environment/world-environment-day-india-can-measure-its-air-now-it-must-learn-how-to-respond)
- In Delhi the app is integrated with 47 agencies. Outside NCR, complaints are forwarded by email to State Pollution Control Boards and can go unnoticed. In Hyderabad, 96 complaints stayed unresolved — [Deccan Chronicle](https://deccanchronicle.com/southern-states/telangana/sameer-app-dysfunctional-complaints-go-unattended-1854571)
- A third-party guide says there is no time frame for when complaints will be addressed — [RTI wiki guide](https://righttoinformation.wiki/air-pollution-complaint-guide-india)
- In 2018, CPCB received more than 3,000 complaints in 10 days, mostly about construction, dumping and open burning of garbage — [HT via PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/india/hindustan-times-lucknow/20181118/282016148364374). In Panipat, app complaints were routed to the municipal corporation (burning) and HSPCB (industry), and HSPCB recommended closure notices to three units — [Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/haryana/three-panipat-units-face-closure-36671)

**Rajasthan Sampark / 181 (pilot state)**
- About 73 lakh cases were registered on helpline 181 from 1 January 2019, and about 71.60 lakh (>98%) were "disposed" (official figure). Disposed does not mean solved — [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/problems-of-people-of-rajasthan-been-halved-says-cm-ashok-gehlot-1150917.html)
- In February 2025 the Chief Secretary directed officials to collect complainant feedback to verify whether grievances were genuinely addressed, to upload photos of resolved complaints, and to keep no case open beyond six months — [SocialNews.xyz](https://www.socialnews.xyz/2025/02/20/rajasthan-chief-secretary-calls-for-timely-resolution-of-complaints-on-sampark-portal/)
- The app allows feedback and reopening, and disposal quality can be rated via 181 (listing last updated September 2023) — [mGov app listing](https://apps.mgov.gov.in/details;jsessionid=2554ACB196BD294D75EC9D71B17C4F75?appid=1988)
- Nine officials were once suspended for laxity in disposing of Jan Sampark complaints — [Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/nation/9-rajasthan-officials-suspended-for-dereliction-of-duty-8034)

**Janaagraha I Change My City (ICMyC) and Praja**
- An ICMyC complaints log for 2019–2022 is published as open data (CC BY-SA) — [OpenCity](https://data.opencity.in/dataset/i-change-my-city-data/resource/i-change-my-city-complaints-log---2019---2022). The AWS Open Data listing is deprecated — [AWS Registry](https://registry.opendata.aws/ichangemycity/)
- No published resolution rate for ICMyC was found.
- Praja Foundation reported over 13,000 escalated citizen complaints still unresolved in 2022 in Mumbai's BMC system — [Praja / HT](https://praja.org/praja_docs/press_images/Hindustan_Times_Mumbai_civic_23_Online_18_May_2023.pdf)

**Reap Benefit Solve Ninja (WhatsApp)**
- About 1.5 lakh young people across 18 states use the WhatsApp chatbot to flag potholes, floods and "garbage black spots", with step-by-step prompts and geotagged photos — [The Better India](https://thebetterindia.com/changemakers/reap-benefit-bengaluru-youth-whatsapp-chatbot-civic-climate-issue-solutions-10516446)
- Self-reported: 50% of Solve Ninjas report problems to local authorities, and 60,000 civic actions. No resolution rate is published — [Give.do](https://give.do:443/discover/17HO/reap-benefit/)
- eGov's DIGIT platform has a WhatsApp (Turn.io) adapter. It creates the grievance in the municipal system, sends the citizen a tracking link, and sends a message on every status change — [DIGIT docs](https://docs.digit.org/local-governance/deploy/configure-digit/services-overview/municipal-services/turn-io-adapter)

**Garbage Vulnerable Points (GVPs): the government's own vocabulary**
- The Swachh Survekshan 2024 toolkit defines GVPs as places where garbage piles up from constant dumping — [SS 2024 toolkit](https://www.bmcgujarat.com/media/mqidez55/ss-2024-toolkit-presentation-19-01-2024.pdf)
- In 2022, UP required geo-tagged before/after photos of each GVP — [HT via PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/india/hindustan-times-west-up/20221201/281582359651371). J&K's 2025 drive required each hotspot to be geo-tagged and root-caused — [J&K SBM action plan](https://swachh.jk.gov.in/mis/storage/app/public/iec/monthly-action-plans/8uZLXBuxuFxPnXxruFzGEphZFP4IBBUYkviFli9L.pdf)
- Mumbai BMC has removed 49 of 231 identified GVPs and installed CCTV at 73 sites, after Bombay HC observations — [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-bmc-eliminates-49-garbage-vulnerable-points-installs-cctv-cameras-at-73-dumping-prone-locations)
- Hyderabad surveyed 1,109 GVPs via a mobile app (2018). Spots declared "eliminated" reappeared — [Deccan Chronicle via PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/india/deccan-chronicle/20180626/281663960740956)

**Global apps**
- FixMyStreet (UK): reports with photos were about 15% more likely to be recorded as fixed than reports without (about 2 percentage points in absolute terms). mySociety cautions that its repair-rate data is not representative — [mySociety 2020](https://www.mysociety.org/2020/11/16/do-photos-help-resolution-of-fixmystreet-reports/)
- mySociety says FixMyStreet data should not be used for national or local comparisons — [FixMyStreet: understanding report data](https://osm.fixmystreet.com/about/understanding-report-data). Residents of more affluent areas are more likely to report, so relying on reports can skew service toward wealthier neighbourhoods — [SocietyWorks](https://www.societyworks.org/research-and-guidance/can-relying-on-citizen-reports-make-neighbourhoods-worse/)
- Sjoberg, Mellon and Peixoto (Public Administration Review 2017; n = 399,364 reports): a successful first FixMyStreet report was associated with a 57% higher probability of submitting a second report. Secondary summaries say 54% — [abstract](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/422186); [World Bank blog](https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/governance/straightforward-way-local-governments-engage-more-their-citizens); [mySociety](https://www.mysociety.org/2015/03/10/local-government-you-can-shape-your-own-responsible-citizens/)
- The ACT (Australia) Legislative Assembly ran an inquiry into "the effectiveness of Fix My Street", with a government response dated June 2026. Its findings were not extracted — [ACT inquiry response](https://www.parliament.act.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0007/3090832/TCS-Report-1-Can-We-Fix-It_-Report-of-the-inquiry-into-the-effectiveness-of-Fix-My-Street-Government-Response.pdf)
- FixMyStreet Pro (vendor-published): after Buckinghamshire moved to it, street-fault calls fell 13% and emails 40% — [SocietyWorks](https://www.societyworks.org/2019/07/04/measuring-the-savings-brought-by-fixmystreet-pro/)
- TrashOut aims to "map and monitor all illegal dumps around the world" and inform authorities and NGOs — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/trashout/id501471692?ls=1). Third-party estimates put the iOS app at about 350 installs and about 13 monthly active users, last updated 30 August 2023 — [AppGoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/501471692)
- Litterati's "City Fingerprint Project" works with Hayward, Norfolk and Memphis, and its "Analyze" ML tool baselines litter, including in illegal-dumping hotspots — [Route Fifty](https://www.route-fifty.com/digital-government/2022/03/how-crowdsourced-data-helps-cities-fight-litter/363361); [Litterati](https://litterati.org/the-city-fingerprint-project)
- A 2024 Vancouver study found that Litterati composition matched municipal audits, but its spatial pattern tracked population more than litter drivers. That is reporting bias — [Environmental Science: Advances 2024](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2024/va/d4va00008k)
- OpenLitterMap offers open data with 100+ litter types, positioned for bin placement and clean-up routes. No enforcement use was found — [data.europa.eu use case](https://data.europa.eu/sites/default/files/use-cases/ireland_-_open_litter_map.pdf)

### Inferences
- Resolution statistics across Indian apps are self-reported closures, and they range from about 58% (Green Delhi 2020) to over 98% (Sampark "disposed"). The Quint field test and Hyderabad's reappearing GVPs show that closure ≠ cleanup. **A feature that independently re-checks a "closed" complaint against later satellite imagery would be novel. I found no app that does this.**
- "Why not just use Swachhata?" Swachhata, 311 and Sampark route to the urban local body (ULB). When the ULB itself runs or tolerates the dump, as in the Jodhpur NGT case (see Q5), a ULB-routed app has a conflict of interest. Saboot's value is escalation to the regulator (RSPCB, CPCB) and the tribunal with documented evidence. It should therefore complement these apps, not compete. Saboot's draft could even quote the Swachhata or Sampark complaint ID as "prior attempt" evidence.
- Saboot drafts should use "GVP" vocabulary, because it maps directly onto Swachh Survekshan scoring, which ULBs care about.
- The global "reporting app" category has weak traction (TrashOut's tiny MAU) and no evidence of enforcement use (Litterati, OpenLitterMap). Judges who know the space will discount another reporting app. The pitch must lead with evidence, rules and follow-up.

### Gaps
- No verified resolution data for ICMyC, MyGov, Solve Ninja, SeeClickFix or the World Cleanup app. These were not researched in depth.
- Play Store review mining for Swachhata or Sampark was not done.
- I found no independent audit of Swachhata fake closures, only journalistic spot checks.
- No municipal WhatsApp bot specific to Jodhpur was found, and Jodhpur Municipal Corporation (North/South) complaint channels were not verified.

## Q2. Which satellite or AI tools exist for waste and environmental monitoring, and do any connect evidence to complaints or legal action?

### Takeaway
Strong precedents exist for satellite waste detection: Global Plastic Watch (Sentinel-2 plus human validation), the EU's PERIVALLON/AerialWaste work (over 90% accuracy, used with ARPA Lombardia), Earth Genome's Earth Index, commercial very-high-resolution services (Airbus/Disaitek, down to about 2 m² piles), and methane plume mapping (Carbon Mapper, Kayrros). They serve researchers, investors and regulators. **None I found hands a citizen a filing-ready complaint.** The closest "evidence → enforcement" loops are in deforestation: Peru's environmental prosecutors use GFW alerts, and Brazil's DETER is linked to a large reduction in deforestation. In India, a state board has submitted Google Earth imagery of dumpsites in an NGT compliance report, but I found no NGT order that relies on satellite imagery.

### Cited Findings

**Earth Genome / Earth Index (Saboot's embedding source)**
- The open embeddings are 384-dimensional vectors from the SoftCon model (Zhu XLabs/TUM) on Sentinel-2 composites. They are GeoParquet with IDs and centroids on a Major TOM-based grid, CC BY 4.0, distributed via Source Cooperative and STAC (`sentinel2-yearly-embeddings`). The blog describes each embedding as covering "a 320 square meter patch". It lists no waste or landfill use cases and makes no quantified accuracy claims — [Earth Genome, "Embeddings for all"](https://www.earthgenome.org/blog/embeddings-for-all); [Source Coop](https://source.coop/earthgenome/earthindexembeddings/2024)
- VectorChord's case study describes more than 3.2 billion overlapping tiles of roughly 10 hectares each. That is consistent with 320 m × 320 m, not 320 m² — [VectorChord](https://docs.vectorchord.ai/vectorchord/customer-stories/earthgenome.html)
- Earth Index lets users highlight an example (deforestation patch, mining site, trawler, airstrip) and find similar patterns elsewhere. Amazon Mining Watch uses it to flag new mining — [Mongabay](https://news.mongabay.com/short-article/a-search-engine-for-the-planet-opens-to-the-public/); [Earth Genome](https://www.earthgenome.org/earth-index)
- Earth Genome deliberately keeps patch-level rather than pixel-level embeddings, noting that pixel-level ones such as Google's AlphaEarth suit land classification. Earth Index has "Open" and "Full" access tiers — [Earth Genome blog](https://www.earthgenome.org/blog/mapping-the-planet-with-earth-index-is-now-open-to-everyone)

**Global Plastic Watch (Minderoo Foundation + Earthrise Media)**
- Two CNNs (a per-pixel spectral classifier and a 28×28 patch classifier) run on Sentinel-2. Trained curators validate every candidate against very-high-resolution Google Earth or Mapbox imagery, and uncertain sites are rejected. In Southeast Asia, only 53% of candidate locations were validated by humans — [Kruse et al., PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0278997)
- Size: 38% of detected sites are smaller than 0.1 ha and 82% smaller than 0.5 ha. Failure modes include plastic greenhouses, haze, turned farm soil, senescent vegetation, and cities and bare earth as "relatively challenging" cover types — [PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0278997)
- 19% of detected sites are within 200 m of a waterway, and more than half within 750 m — [PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0278997)
- At its 2022 launch, coverage reported 690 sites in India (415 in China), updated monthly, with risk indicators by proximity to water or communities — [Acre](https://www.acre.com/blog/2022/05/the-worlds-plastic-waste-you-can-see-from-space); [The Engineer](https://www.theengineer.co.uk/content/news/ai-tool-tracks-plastic-waste-from-space). A timeline view shows a cleanup in Pakur, Jharkhand — [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/global-plastic-watch-la-technologie-satellite-au-profit-de-la-lutte-contre-la-pollution-10992184)

**EU / commercial**
- Politecnico di Milano's PERIVALLON project (Horizon Europe; partners include ARPA Lombardia and the Carabinieri environmental unit) reports over 90% accuracy in identifying illegal dumping sites, using the public AerialWaste dataset (6,500+ downloads). A pilot with ARPA Lombardia covered 100+ municipalities. No enforcement outcomes were reported — [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1091286)
- Airbus/Disaitek combine Pléiades Neo imagery with AI to find waste piles as small as about 2 m² (commercial) — [Airbus case study](https://space-solutions.airbus.com/resources/case-studies/defence/illegal-dumping-detection-monitoring/)
- In Oaxaca, a Random Forest on Sentinel-2 reported about 95% accuracy but struggled to separate garbage from bare or urban soil — [Cuadernos de Investigación Geográfica](https://publicaciones.unirioja.es/ojs/index.php/cig/article/view/6273)

**Methane (landfill-scale only)**
- The UCLA STOP Methane analysis of Carbon Mapper and GHGSat data covered nearly 3,000 plumes from 700+ waste sites. Jawaharnagar (Hyderabad) ranked 4th globally at about 5.9 t CH4/h, and a Mumbai landfill was about 4.9 t/h. Dates are reported inconsistently (2025 vs 2026) — [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/news/telangana-maharashtra-landfills-methane-super-emitters-2025); [Climate Fact Checks](https://climatefactchecks.org/invisible-methane-hotspots-satellite-data-reveals-indias-landfill-emissions/)
- Kayrros found that India had more methane clouds than any country except the US in H1 2022 — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/features/2022-methane-landfills-south-asia-climate-health-hazard/). Ghazipur methane time series exist from EMIT and EnMAP hyperspectral data — [Zhang, IWGGMS poster](https://atmosphere.copernicus.eu/sites/default/files/custom-uploads/IWGGMS%2022%20event/Day4/6.06_Zhang.pdf)

**India: government inventories and use of imagery**
- The SBM-U 2.0 legacy-waste programme counts dumpsites holding more than 1,000 tonnes: 2,429 sites with 23.57 crore MT were identified (Lok Sabha reply, December 2024) — [Lok Sabha AS309](https://eparlib.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/2982283/1/AS309_kW2GKS.pdf). By November 2025, 1,048 of 2,476 dumpsites were fully remediated — [PIB concept note](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc2025118687601.pdf). In January 2026, over 61% of legacy waste had been processed, with 214 high-impact sites holding about 80% of what remains — [PIB Jan 2026](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2026/jan/doc2026131775201.pdf)
- The Dumpsite Remediation Accelerator Programme (DRAP), launched in November 2025, has a "Lakshya Zero Dumpsites" target of September or October 2026; sources differ — [VisionIAS](https://visionias.in/current-affairs/news-today/2025-11-11/environment/dumpsite-remediation-accelerator-programme-drap-launched); [PIB Jan 2026](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2026/jan/doc2026131775201.pdf)
- In OA 519/2019 (legacy waste), the NGT directed CPCB to compile dumpsite data and post it online. Kerala SPCB submitted an updated dumpsite list "along with a map showing the location of dumpsites along with its Google earth imagery" and was conducting drone surveys — [India Environment Portal](https://indiaenvironmentportal.org.in/in-court/report-on-landfills-in-kerala-01012020)
- In OA 668/2023 (Punjab), an applicant attached a satellite image showing the site's location — [NGT filing](https://www.greentribunal.gov.in/sites/default/files/news_updates/Reply%20of%20Applicant%20in%20OA%20No.%20668%20of%202023%20(Parasher%20Dev%20sharma%20Vs.%20State%20of%20Punjab%20&%20Ors.).pdf). The NGT has asked states to verify claims made through citizens' photographs, for example in Uttarakhand — [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/news-updates/ngt-asks-ukhand-to-verify-claims-made-through-photographs-of-chaotic-waste-disposal-read-order-147576)
- Down To Earth used Landsat 5/9 (2013–24) to quantify the volume and surface-temperature change of Delhi's Ghazipur, Okhla and Bhalswa dumps — [DTE](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/waste/overview-helps)
- Uttarakhand IT is mapping plastic dump sites along the Char Dham route and around Dehradun with high-resolution imagery and GIS — [ITDA Uttarakhand](https://it.uk.gov.in/?p=6940)

**Evidence → enforcement precedents (forests)**
- In Peru, the environmental prosecutor's office (FEMA) uses deforestation alerts to identify potential illegal activity and confirm other evidence in legal cases. Uptake depended on clear jurisdiction and a few influential early adopters — [WRI issue brief](https://www.wri.org/publication/use-near-real-time-deforestation-alerts); [GFW blog](https://www.globalforestwatch.org/blog/users-in-action/triumphs-and-challenges-of-using-deforestation-alerts-in-peru/)
- In Brazil, deforestation observed in 2007–2011 was estimated at 75% lower than without DETER-targeted fines — [PUC-Rio working paper](https://www.econ.puc-rio.br/uploads/adm/trabalhos/files/Command_and_Control.pdf)
- In Aceh, HAkA coordinates field investigations from GFW alerts. Slides credit it with more enforcement cases (no stated method) — [GFOI slides](https://gfoi-rd.gfz.de/fileadmin/gfoird/documents/2025_06_LPS/Presentation4.pdf). A 2021 legal study found that Aceh officers used before/after satellite imagery in illegal-logging cases — [Fiat Justisia via DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/2c2a9cc8f80e4d2eba834c4fe173ed22)

**NASA FIRMS (fire layer) limits**
- VIIRS 375 m can detect fires smaller than a pixel, but a "fire" pixel doesn't mean the whole pixel is burning. Detection depends on overpass timing, clouds and smoke, and validation of the minimum detectable fire size was still under way — [NWCG VIIRS metadata](https://fsapps.nwcg.gov/afm/data_viirs_iband/fireptdata/viirs_iband_fire_2016_conus.htm)
- The 375 m product detects about 3× (day) and about 25× (night) more fire pixels than the 750 m baseline — [Schroeder et al. 2014, via NASA](https://earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/imported/Schroeder_et_al_2014b_RSE.pdf)
- VIIRS I-band detects small fires (FRP ≤ 1 MW) but produces false alarms near man-made structures, mitigated with land-cover and OSM masks — [Remote Sensing 2021](https://doaj.org/article/d6e94ba670eb4232bf954a68b48c1865)
- In FIREX-AQ (preliminary), about 41% of small ground-verified fires were missed by GOES, VIIRS and MODIS — [EGU2020 presentation](https://presentations.copernicus.org/EGU2020/EGU2020-3076_presentation.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Earth Genome's "320 m" means a patch about 320 m on a side (about 10 ha).** The blog's "320 square meter" wording is almost certainly a slip (32×32 Sentinel-2 pixels at 10 m is 320 m). With 82% of GPW-detected waste sites under 0.5 ha, a typical dump fills less than 5% of a Saboot patch, so similarity search will mostly retrieve large dumps, landfills and transfer stations, plus look-alike land covers. In arid Jodhpur, bare soil, construction sites and quarries are the likely false positives (GPW and Oaxaca both flag bare soil). The team should present lookalikes as **candidates for human review**, not detections, and quote a measured precision (see improvement #4).
- The official legacy-dumpsite inventory only covers sites over 1,000 tonnes. **Smaller, newer illegal dumps and burn spots fall outside the national tracking system**, which is a clean, defensible niche for Saboot.
- DRAP's "zero dumpsites" deadline falls in September or October 2026, the same month as the hackathon. Saboot can be framed as a citizen check on that target: "the government says zero dumpsites by October 2026; here is what the satellite shows in Jodhpur."
- Peru's lesson is that alerts get used when a specific institution with jurisdiction adopts them. For Saboot, that suggests designing the output for RSPCB's regional office and the NGT format, not just for the ULB.
- Methane and CO2e features are only meaningful at mega-landfills (several tonnes per hour). They add little for Jodhpur-scale illegal dumps and risk false precision.

### Gaps
- Not verified in this pass: SkyTruth (only its 2019 AWS re:Invent bilge-dumping challenge surfaced), Blue Sky Analytics, SatSure, ISRO Bhuvan Swachh Bharat layers, and the CPCB or NGT use of drones beyond the Kerala mention.
- No geotagged public layer of SBM-U legacy dumpsites was confirmed. If one exists, it would be a ready ground-truth set.
- GPW's current India or Rajasthan site counts were not retrieved (only the 2022 launch figure).
- No study was found that measures Earth Genome embedding performance on waste sites.

## Q3. Which AI legal or complaint-drafting tools exist in India?

### Takeaway
India's AI grievance tools focus on language access and routing (Samadhan Didi on CPGRAMS, Jugalbandi on Bhashini) or on consumer complaints (Grahak-Nyay). None I found retrieves environmental rules, attaches geospatial evidence, or targets SPCB or NGT filings. Saboot's "cited rule from a retrieved document" is a real differentiator, and Samadhan Didi is a precedent for automatically identifying the responsible authority.

### Cited Findings
- Samadhan Didi is a voice bot on CPGRAMS built by DARPG with BHASHINI. ## Q5. What would a skeptical judge ask, and what are the evidence-based answers or mitigations?

### Takeaway
Saboot's strongest answers are (a) the Jodhpur NGT case, which shows exactly why a rule-cited, evidence-backed complaint with follow-up matters, (b) BSA Section 63(4)'s hash requirement, which makes the evidence-pack idea legally grounded, and (c) the gap in the official dumpsite inventory (sites over 1,000 t only). Its weakest point is lookalike precision on 10 ha patches in an arid district. That needs a measured number, not a claim.

### Cited Findings

**J1. "Can 320 m embeddings really find dumps?"**
- The patch is about 10 ha [VectorChord](https://docs.vectorchord.ai/vectorchord/customer-stories/earthgenome.html), and Earth Genome publishes no waste accuracy [Earth Genome](https://www.earthgenome.org/blog/embeddings-for-all). Most detected waste sites are under 0.5 ha [PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0278997). Even a purpose-built Sentinel-2 pipeline had only 53% of candidates confirmed by humans in Southeast Asia [PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0278997). Bare and urban soil is a known confuser [Oaxaca study](https://publicaciones.unirioja.es/ojs/index.php/cig/article/view/6273).
- *Mitigation:* report precision@k on a hand-labelled Jodhpur set, call results "candidates", keep a human-review step (GPW does this too), and use the pin's own 10 m Sentinel-2 pixels for site-level change.

**J2. "Will authorities act on satellite evidence?"**
- In India, no NGT order relying on satellite imagery was found. But imagery has entered NGT records via Kerala SPCB's Google Earth maps [IEP](https://indiaenvironmentportal.org.in/in-court/report-on-landfills-in-kerala-01012020) and an applicant's satellite image in OA 668/2023 [NGT](https://www.greentribunal.gov.in/sites/default/files/news_updates/Reply%20of%20Applicant%20in%20OA%20No.%20668%20of%202023%20(Parasher%20Dev%20sharma%20Vs.%20State%20of%20Punjab%20&%20Ors.).pdf). The NGT typically orders verification of photo claims [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/news-updates/ngt-asks-ukhand-to-verify-claims-made-through-photographs-of-chaotic-waste-disposal-read-order-147576).
- Abroad, prosecutors use satellite alerts as corroboration [WRI](https://www.wri.org/publication/use-near-real-time-deforestation-alerts), and DETER-targeted fines are associated with large deforestation reductions [PUC-Rio](https://www.econ.puc-rio.br/uploads/adm/trabalhos/files/Command_and_Control.pdf).
- *Mitigation:* present satellite as **corroboration of duration and scale** ("visible since March 2023, grew X%"), paired with the resident's ground photo, and phrase it as a request to inspect and verify.

**J3. "Is this even admissible?"**
- BSA Section 63(4) requires a certificate in the prescribed format stating the record's hash value and the hash function (MD5, SHA-256, SHA-512). Part A is filled by the person producing the record, and Part B is labelled "to be filled by the expert" [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/articles/hash-value-electronic-evidences-535457); [Bhatt & Joshi](https://bhattandjoshiassociates.com/importance-of-hash-value-in-the-context-of-digital-evidence-collection/)
- On 22 May 2026 the Supreme Court rejected the Pune Bar Association's challenge to S.63(4), and left open the scope of Part B expert certification [SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2026/07/08/sc-upholds-admissibility-of-electronic-evidence-under-section-63-4-bsa/); [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/top-stories/supreme-court-rejects-challenge-to-s634-bsa-mandating-hash-value-disclosure-for-electronic-evidence-535950)
- Delay in generating the hash weakens admissibility [Bhatt & Joshi](https://bhattandjoshiassociates.com/importance-of-hash-value-in-the-context-of-digital-evidence-collection/). A compliant certificate does not by itself establish authenticity or weight [JSA, June 2026](https://www.jsalaw.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/JSA-Prism-Dispute-Resolution-June-2026-Electronic-Evidence.Final_.pdf)
- *Mitigation:* hash at capture time, record source scene IDs, and generate a Part A helper sheet. Clearly state that Part B and filing need a human or advocate.

**J4. "What happens after filing?" (the Jodhpur story)**
- **EA 3/2024 in OA 54/2023 (NGT Central Zone, Bhopal), "Ajay Singh Kachawah vs State of Rajasthan".** The applicant alleged that Nagar Nigam Jodhpur set up a municipal waste dumping site inside a residential area, although JDA allotted land at village Geva (Khasra 680/1, 680/2, 681/1) for a dump on 30 December 2019. He also alleged that RSPCB took no action.
  - On 21 July 2023 the NGT disposed of the OA, holding that "in absence of any violation of any statutory rule in establishing dumping yard at the place in question, we do not find that any right is vested in the applicant". It directed the Commissioner, Nagar Nigam Jodhpur, to examine the matter with RSPCB and residents within 2 months.
  - On 11 March 2024 the applicant filed an execution application, stating that "even after passing of more than 08 months no action has been taken". Annexure A/1 contains photographs of the illegal dumping — [NGT EA 3/2024 filing](https://www.greentribunal.gov.in/sites/default/files/news_updates/E.A.%20No.%2003-2024%20AJAY%20SINGH%20KACHAWAH%20VS%20STATE%20OF%20RAJASTHAN%20%26%20ORS.pdf) (read directly, pages 1–4)
- A separate NGT matter (OA 31/2025 (CZ), Sarwar Khan vs State of Rajasthan) led to a joint RSPCB and Jodhpur Municipal Corporation inspection on 18 April 2025 of the corporation's transfer station and dumping yard behind Central Jail, including ambient air monitoring. The scanned text's findings were not readable — [NGT joint committee report](https://www.greentribunal.gov.in/sites/default/files/news_updates/JCR.pdf)
- Sameer has no complaint time frame [RTI wiki](https://righttoinformation.wiki/air-pollution-complaint-guide-india), and outside NCR it relies on email forwarding [Deccan Chronicle](https://deccanchronicle.com/southern-states/telangana/sameer-app-dysfunctional-complaints-go-unattended-1854571).
- RTI Act: the PIO must reply within 30 days (S.7(1)); silence is a deemed refusal (S.7(2)); first appeal goes to the First Appellate Authority within 30 days (S.19(1)), with a decision ordinarily in 30 days, extendable to 45; second appeal goes to the Information Commission within 90 days. A PIO can be fined ₹250/day — [Vikaspedia](https://en.vikaspedia.in/viewcontent/e-governance/about-rti-act-2005/first-appeal-under-rti-what-to-do-when-there-is-no-reply?lgn=en); [iPleaders](https://blog.ipleaders.in/?p=36129); [Goa SIC decision](https://gsic.goa.gov.in/decision/2023/Appeal%20No.%20%20%2070-2023-sic.pdf)
- People whose first report gets fixed are about 57% more likely to report again [PAR abstract](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/422186)
- *Mitigation:* a deadline tracker, an escalation ladder, an auto-drafted RTI, and satellite closure verification.

**J5. "Who is the user, and why not Swachhata?"**
- Swachhata's model is quick ULB service tickets (12-hour SLA) [IEP](https://www.indiaenvironmentportal.org.in/news-clippings/swachh-app-a-hit-10l-issues-sorted-out-in-10-months). In the Jodhpur case, the ULB itself ran the contested dump [NGT EA 3/2024](https://www.greentribunal.gov.in/sites/default/files/news_updates/E.A.%20No.%2003-2024%20AJAY%20SINGH%20KACHAWAH%20VS%20STATE%20OF%20RAJASTHAN%20%26%20ORS.pdf).
- Youth civic volunteers (Solve Ninja's 1.5 lakh) are an existing user base for evidence-gathering [Better India](https://thebetterindia.com/changemakers/reap-benefit-bengaluru-youth-whatsapp-chatbot-civic-climate-issue-solutions-10516446).
- *Answer:* Saboot is for persistent, large or recurring dumps and burn sites where a ULB ticket has failed or the ULB is the operator. Its users are RWAs, local environmental activists and petitioners, student volunteers, and journalists. It works after or alongside Swachhata, and can cite the Swachhata or Sampark ticket as a prior attempt.

**J6. "How do you prevent misuse or false accusations?"**
- Reporting data skews to affluent or populous areas [SocietyWorks](https://www.societyworks.org/research-and-guidance/can-relying-on-citizen-reports-make-neighbourhoods-worse/); [Env. Sci. Advances](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2024/va/d4va00008k). Satellite imagery can't identify who is responsible (Saboot synopsis).
- *Mitigation:* never name a person (already in the design); blur faces and number plates in uploaded photos; flag likely private land; require that a photo's EXIF location and time match the pin; label confidence per evidence item; rate-limit; keep manual filing. For hotspot maps, show exposure-weighted risk, not report counts.

**J7. "Is open burning even a big deal compared with vehicles or industry?"**
- WRI India (11 cities) found that open waste burning is under 1% of citywide PM2.5 and PM10 emissions but sharply raises short-term exposure near homes and dumps, and incidence roughly triples in winter. This comes from a secondary summary; the WRI original was not retrieved — [SuperKalam summary](https://superkalam.com/current-affairs/10-09-2026/pollution-from-open-waste-burning-triples-in-cities-during-winter-study-pg10-5678da0d-27ae-4dcb-ac3a-a75acfb04571)
- In Delhi, residential biomass burning was the largest PM2.5 source, followed by open burning of trash and biomass (IIT Bombay, HEI and IHME, 2018) — [IEP](https://indiaenvironmentportal.org.in/news-clippings/residential-biomass-burning-open-waste-burning-responsible-for-highest-pm-25-exposure-study)
- In Agra, open waste burning plus dung cake burning was linked to an estimated 713 (377–1,050) premature deaths per year, dominated by waste burning in lower-status neighbourhoods — [Georgia Tech](https://drupaldev.ce.gatech.edu/news/2016/10/burning-trash-india-major-cause-taj-mahal-discoloration-leads-hundreds-premature)
- Delhi recorded 24–130 waste-burning incidents per km² per day in winter, highest in low-SES neighbourhoods — [UMN](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/characterizing-the-spatial-and-temporal-patterns-of-open-burning-/)
- *Answer:* frame Saboot as an **exposure and environmental-justice** tool, not a citywide AQI fix.

**J8. "Are the 2026 rules really what you cite?"**
- The SWM Rules 2026 were notified as S.O. 388(E) on 27 January 2026, effective 1 April 2026, replacing the 2016 rules — [Kamrup Metro notice](https://kamrupmetro.assam.gov.in/information-and-services/implementation-solid-waste-management-swm-rules-2026-under-kamrup-metropolitan-district); [Vajiram](https://vajiramandravi.com/current-affairs/solid-waste-management-rules-2026/)
- Rule 17 provides polluter-pays environmental compensation: CPCB issues guidelines and SPCBs levy it. The Supreme Court has directed MoEFCC to frame guidelines for imposing and collecting it — [Vajiram](https://vajiramandravi.com/current-affairs/solid-waste-management-rules-2026/); [Neeti Niyaman](https://neetiniyaman.com/supreme-court-order-on-solid-waste-management-rules-2026/)
- ULBs must appoint a solid-waste nodal officer, report monthly on a central portal, and align by-laws by 31 March 2027 (as quoted in an Assam notice citing Rule 39) — [Kamrup Metro](https://kamrupmetro.assam.gov.in/information-and-services/implementation-solid-waste-management-swm-rules-2026-under-kamrup-metropolitan-district)
- The NGT's December 2016 order banned open burning of waste, including at landfills, with compensation of ₹5,000 for simple burning and ₹25,000 for bulk burning — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/article/news-ians/ngt-orders-enforcement-of-waste-management-rules-bans-open-burning-of-waste-116122301087_1.html)

### Inferences
- **The Jodhpur NGT case is the single best narrative hook.** The tribunal declined relief for lack of a cited statutory violation, and a 2-month direction went unimplemented for more than 8 months. Saboot's two core features address exactly these two failure points: a cited rule, and evidence of persistence plus follow-up. Use the public case reference rather than foregrounding the individual.
- The NGT 2016 burning-compensation amounts (₹5,000 and ₹25,000) are concrete numbers the draft can cite as precedent, but the agent must label them as an NGT order, not an SWM 2026 clause.
- Rule-retrieval risk: secondary sources disagree on SWM 2026 details. The RAG corpus should use the gazette PDF (S.O. 388(E)) only, and the demo should show the retrieved clause text next to the draft.

### Gaps
- The exact SWM 2026 clause numbers for open burning or dumping prohibitions, and any statutory complaint-response timeline, were not verified from the gazette text.
- The outcome of EA 3/2024 and the findings of the OA 31/2025 joint inspection could not be read; scanned PDFs need OCR.
- No direct evidence was found that RSPCB Jodhpur acts on emailed or app complaints.
