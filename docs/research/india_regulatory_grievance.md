## 1. Title, notification number and date, effective date, and changes from 2016

### Takeaway
The rules are the **Solid Waste Management Rules, 2026**, notified by MoEFCC as **S.O. 388(E) dated 27 January 2026**. Under **rule 1(2)** they came into force on **1 April 2026**, which confirms the team's date. The notification is an S.O. number, not a G.S.R. number. The 2026 Rules supersede the 2016 Rules. The main changes are:
- four-stream segregation, replacing three streams
- a defined "bulk waste generator" with Extended Bulk Waste Generator Responsibility (EBWGR) certificates
- a CPCB centralised online portal
- an explicit environmental compensation rule (rule 17)
- dated dumpsite-mapping and legacy-waste duties
- a mandatory grievance mechanism for local bodies

### Cited Findings
- **Notification and date [FINAL 2026]:** The Gazette notification is dated "New Delhi, the 27th January 2026" and numbered "S.O. 388(E)". It was published in Gazette of India, Extraordinary, Part II, Section 3, Sub-section (ii) — [Gazette text, MPCB copy](https://www.mpcb.gov.in/sites/default/files/standing_orders/SWM_2026.pdf); also recorded as S.O. 388(E) dated 27.01.2026 in [OpenCity dataset](https://data.opencity.in/dataset/solid-waste-management-rules-2026).
- **Title and commencement (rule 1):** "These rules may be called the Solid Waste Management Rules, 2026" (rule 1(1)). "They shall come into force with effect from the 1st day of April 2026" (rule 1(2)) — [Gazette text](https://www.mpcb.gov.in/sites/default/files/standing_orders/SWM_2026.pdf).
- **Legal basis and supersession:** The rules were made under **sections 3, 6 and 25 of the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986**. They supersede the SWM Rules, 2016, "except as respect of things done or omitted to be done before such supersession". The Gazette misprints "supersession" as "suppression" — [Gazette text](https://www.mpcb.gov.in/sites/default/files/standing_orders/SWM_2026.pdf).
- **Draft history [DRAFT 2024]:**
  - The draft was the "Solid Waste Management Rules, 2024", notified as **S.O. 5369(E) dated 9 December 2024**. Copies were made public on 14 December 2024, with a 60-day window for objections.
  - The final rules were issued after objections were considered — [Gazette preamble](https://www.mpcb.gov.in/sites/default/files/standing_orders/SWM_2026.pdf).
- **Application (rule 2):** The rules apply to every urban and rural local body and to all entities in their jurisdiction. This includes:
  - special notified areas, notified industrial areas and townships, SEZs, and food parks
  - Indian Railways areas
  - (per summaries) airports, ports, and defence establishments
  - [Gazette text](https://www.mpcb.gov.in/sites/default/files/standing_orders/SWM_2026.pdf); [Vikaspedia summary](https://en.vikaspedia.in/viewcontent/energy/environment/waste-management/solid-waste-management-rules-2026).
- **Change: four streams, up from three.**
  - **2026 (rule 5(1)(b)):** Generators must segregate at source into four streams: "wet waste, dry waste, sanitary waste and special care waste" — [Gazette text](https://www.mpcb.gov.in/sites/default/files/standing_orders/SWM_2026.pdf).
  - **[2016, SUPERSEDED]:** The 2016 rules required three streams: biodegradable, non-biodegradable, and domestic hazardous — [Down To Earth on 2016 rules](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/waste/solid-waste-management-rules-2016-53443); [NPC SWM toolkit](https://npcindia.gov.in/NPC/Files/delhiOFC/EM/Toolkit-kit-on-Solid-waste-management.pdf).
- **Change: bulk waste generator (BWG) definition and EBWGR.**
  - **Rule 3(1)(i)** defines a BWG as an institutional or commercial entity meeting at least one of these criteria:
    - floor area of 20,000 sq m or more
    - water consumption of 40,000 litres per day
    - solid waste generation of 100 kg per day
  - **Rule 6** requires BWGs to:
    - register on the centralised portal (6(a))
    - process wet waste on site, or buy EBWGR certificates (6(c)–(f))
    - file annual returns by 30 June (6(h))
  - [Gazette text](https://www.mpcb.gov.in/sites/default/files/standing_orders/SWM_2026.pdf); [Indian Express summary via Vision IAS](https://dce.visionias.in/upsc-daily-news-summary/article/2026-01-29/the-indian-express/environment/centre-notifies-new-solid-waste-management-rules-places-larger-onus-on-bulk-generators).
- **Change: centralised online portal (rule 16).**
  - CPCB must run a portal for registration and annual returns "within six months of commencement", i.e. by about 1 October 2026.
  - The portal dashboard must make public, at national, state and district level, the data on:
    - solid waste generated, collected, processed and landfilled (16(2))
    - sanitary landfills and legacy dumpsites (16(3))
  - [Gazette text](https://www.mpcb.gov.in/sites/default/files/standing_orders/SWM_2026.pdf).
  - Secondary sources report the portal is live at swm.cpcb.gov.in and BWGs have been asked to register — [Lawrbit](https://www.lawrbit.com/article/solid-waste-management-rules-2026/). I did not verify this on the CPCB site.
- **Change: environmental compensation codified (rule 17).** See Q2. One secondary comparison table places EC under "Rule 21". That is wrong: in the Gazette, rule 21 is "Accident reporting" and rule 17 is "Imposition of Environmental Compensation" — [Gazette text](https://www.mpcb.gov.in/sites/default/files/standing_orders/SWM_2026.pdf).
- **Other changes (from PIB-derived summaries):**
  - circular-economy and EPR principles, with a "zero waste to landfill" aim
  - faster land allocation for facilities
  - RDF substitution in industry
  - State-level committees chaired by the Chief Secretary
  - [InsightsIAS summary citing PIB](https://www.insightsonindia.com/2026/01/29/solid-waste-management-swm-rules-2026/).
  - **Rule 11 (RDF):** Industrial units using solid fuel within specified distances of RDF plants must replace part of that fuel with RDF or segregated combustible fraction — [Gazette text](https://www.mpcb.gov.in/sites/default/files/standing_orders/SWM_2026.pdf).
- **Criticism:** Critics cite excessive centralisation and compliance demands that may be impractical for states and local bodies — [Vikaspedia / search summary](https://en.vikaspedia.in/viewcontent/energy/environment/waste-management/solid-waste-management-rules-2026). This is secondary commentary.

### Inferences
- In complaint text and the retrieval corpus, cite the rules as "Solid Waste Management Rules, 2026, notified vide S.O. 388(E) dated 27.01.2026, in force from 01.04.2026". Do not cite a G.S.R. number.
- Do not index the 2024 draft (S.O. 5369(E)) as operative law. Draft rule numbering may differ from the final.
- I believe the 2016 Rules had no standalone EC rule; EC for waste violations came mainly from NGT orders such as OA 199/2014 and OA 606/2018. I did not verify this against the 2016 text, so treat it as inference.

## 2. Prohibitions, duties, penalties, environmental compensation and timelines (with rule numbers)

### Takeaway
The 2026 Rules prohibit throwing, burning or burying waste in public spaces, drains and water bodies (rule 5(1)(f)). They require local bodies to stop littering, burning and burying (rule 39(7)), and require gram panchayats to ensure "open dumping and burning of solid waste does not take place" (rule 40(3)(iii)(d)). Local bodies must map all dumpsites and "garbage vulnerable points" by **31 October 2026** (rule 15(1)). Enforcement works mainly through three routes:
- **Environmental compensation levied by the SPCB** (rules 17 and 41(4)). Rule 41(4) expressly covers "indiscriminate dumping of solid waste by local authorities". CPCB guidelines on EC amounts are still pending.
- **Penalties under s.15 of the EP Act** (rule 17(5)).
- **Spot fines under municipal bye-laws** (rule 39(31), 39(37)).

The rules themselves set no rupee amounts.

### Cited Findings
All rule text below is [FINAL 2026], quoted or paraphrased from the [Gazette text](https://www.mpcb.gov.in/sites/default/files/standing_orders/SWM_2026.pdf) unless another source is given.

**Prohibitions**
- **Rule 5(1)(f):** Every waste generator shall "not throw, burn or bury the solid waste generated by them, on streets, open public spaces outside his premises or dispose in the drain or water bodies".
- **Rule 5(1)(j):** No event of more than 100 persons at an unlicensed place without telling the local body at least 3 working days ahead. The organiser must ensure segregation.
- **Rule 39(7):** All local bodies "shall direct waste generators not to litter i.e throw or dispose of any waste ... or burn or bury waste on streets, open public spaces, drains, waste [water] bodies".
- **Rule 39(15):** ULBs shall direct street sweepers "not to burn tree leaves collected from street sweeping".
- **Rule 40(3)(iii)(d):** The Gram Panchayat shall ensure "that open dumping and burning of solid waste does not take place".
- **Rule 14(4) (landfill restriction):** Only non-usable, non-recyclable, non-biodegradable, non-combustible, non-energy-recoverable dry waste, inerts, and processing rejects or residues may go to a sanitary landfill. "No wet waste or construction and demolition waste shall be disposed in the sanitary landfill".
  - A weight-based landfill user fee applies to unsegregated or recyclable waste sent there until processing capacity exists.
  - After that, unsegregated waste "cannot be disposed of in the sanitary landfill".
- **Rule 15(7):** No fresh waste or C&D waste may be dumped on legacy dumpsites under biomining or bioremediation.
- **Definition, rule 3(1)(t):** "dump sites" means land used by a local body for waste disposal "without following the principles of sanitary land filling".

**Dumpsites and legacy waste (rule 15)**
- **15(1):** All existing dumpsites and "garbage vulnerable points" must be geographically mapped and assessed "by 31 October 2026" in urban and rural areas, by the local body. The results must be published on the centralised portal and the local body website.
- **15(2):** All legacy dumpsites are to be biomined and bioremediated "as practicable". Progress must be updated quarterly, by the 30th of the first month of the next quarter.
- **15(3):** Local bodies must prepare a time-bound action plan per CPCB legacy-waste guidelines and upload it to the portal.
- **15(4)(v):** Fire-prevention measures must follow **Schedule IV**.
- **15(8):** Biomining is to be verified through a third party authorised by the SPCB, with quarterly returns.
- **15(9):** The SPCB enforces the norms.
- **15(10):** The SPCB or a Registered Environment Auditor must do an annual audit, uploaded by 31 December of the next financial year.
- **Schedule IV (fire prevention at dumpsites) requires:**
  - on-site emergency plans integrated with district disaster management plans by the District Collector
  - landfill gas detectors
  - temperature monitoring
  - "Installation of CCTV cameras at the site and provision of fencing & frequent patrolling"
  - quarterly landfill gas monitoring reports from the ULB to the SPCB

**Duties of generators and bulk generators**
- **Rule 5:** General generator duties. These include four-stream segregation, user fees (5(1)(h)), and C&D waste handling under the Environment (Construction and Demolition) Waste Management Rules, 2025 (5(1)(d)).
- **Rule 5(2):** Gated communities and institutions above 5,000 sq m, RWAs, market associations, hotels and restaurants must ensure source segregation and on-site wet-waste processing "within one year from the date of notification".
- **Rule 6:** BWG duties (see Q1). Also rule 6(g): BWGs must not engage unregistered entities.

**Duties of urban local bodies (rule 39)**
- **39(2):** A solid waste action plan within one year of the state policy. It must include:
  - "(vii) plan for collection and transportation of waste from garbage vulnerable points and hot spots for littering"
  - "(xi) mapping of vacant plots of land under public or private ownership vulnerable for open dumping of solid waste including railway land and land with public authorities"
- **39(8):** Geo-tagging of SWM infrastructure.
- **39(9):** 100% coverage.
- **39(12):** Appoint a **nodal officer** for SWM.
- **39(31):** Prescribe criteria for **spot fines** on bulk generators and "persons who litters or fails to comply". Delegate fining powers under bye-laws.
- **39(34):** ULBs with population above 5 lakh must set up **central control rooms** for digital monitoring (GPS tracking, infrastructure mapping), per Schedule I.
- **39(35):** "Urban Local bodies shall establish a grievance redressal mechanism for solid waste management including by online mode within a period of one year from date of notification". Grievance status goes in annual returns by 30 June.
- **39(36):** Identify and geo-tag BWGs every year by 1 April.
- **39(37):** Notify "suitable fine provisions in the by-laws".

**Duties of rural local bodies and gram panchayats (rule 40)**
- **40(2)(xv):** Rural local bodies must likewise set up an online grievance mechanism within one year.
- **40(2)(j):** Map vacant plots vulnerable to open dumping.
- **40(3):** The Gram Panchayat must ensure SWM at village level, including no open dumping or burning.

**Duties of the District Magistrate / Collector (rule 37)**
- (i) Identify and allocate land for facilities.
- (ii) "review the performance of local bodies, at least once in a quarter" on segregation, collection, processing and disposal. Take corrective measures and upload the review minutes.
- (iv) The DC or SDM may inspect or audit facilities. The DC "shall ensure that the sanitary landfill is operated as per provisions of these rules".

**Duties of the SPCB (rule 41)**
- **41(1)(i):** "enforce these rules in their State and review the implementation ... at least twice a year".
- **41(1)(xvi)–(xvii):** Ensure compliance by registered entities and verify it through inspection and audit.
- **41(1)(xxii):** Audit ULB and panchayat annual reports and publish them.
- **41(3):** Physically verify SWM activities of local authorities.
- **41(4):** "shall impose Environmental Compensation based upon polluter pays principle, on persons who are not complying with the provisions of these rules, as per guidelines notified by the Central Pollution Control Board including for indiscriminate dumping of solid waste by local authorities".
- **41(8):** Geo-tagging of SWM infrastructure.

**Duties of CPCB (rule 38)**
- Coordinate the SPCBs.
- Monitor implementation by local bodies through the SPCBs (38(1)(v)).
- Publish an annual report on implementation by **30 September** each year (38(1)(vi)).

**Environmental compensation (rule 17)**
- **17(1):** EC is "levied based upon polluter pays principle on persons, who are not complying with the provisions of these rules". This expressly includes:
  - (a) operating without registration
  - (b) false information
  - (c) forged documents
  - (d) entities engaged in collection, segregation, sorting, transport, processing and disposal
- **17(2):** The Central Implementation Committee (constituted by CPCB under rule 18) prepares the EC guidelines.
- **17(3):** "The environment compensation shall be levied by the concerned State Pollution Control Board". If the SPCB "does not take action in reasonable time, the Central Pollution Control Board shall issue directions to the State Pollution Control Board".
- **17(4):** EC funds are held in an escrow account and used for SWM.
- **17(5):** "In addition ... action under section 15 of the Environment (Protection) Act 1986, may also be taken."

**Status of the EC guidelines (as of the latest reports)**
- In **Amravati Municipal Corporation v. Ganesh Dadarao Anasane** (SC, judgment of 4 August 2026; 2026 INSC 796 / 2026 LiveLaw (SC) 760; Justices P.S. Narasimha and Alok Aradhe), the Supreme Court directed MoEFCC to frame EC guidelines under **Rule 17(2) of the SWM Rules 2026** — [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/top-stories/solid-waste-management-rules-2026-supreme-court-directs-centre-to-frame-guidelines-on-environmental-compensation-544325); [Verdictum](https://www.verdictum.in/supreme-court/amravati-municipal-corporation-v-ganesh-dadarao-anasane-2026-insc-796-1619266); [Bar & Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/news/litigation/solid-waste-rule-violations-supreme-court-asks-centre-to-frame-compensation-guidelines-lays-down-8-principles).
- The Court laid down 8 principles, including:
  - EC is restitutionary and in addition to penalties
  - the obligation lasts until the damage is reversed
  - EC must be proportional and reasoned
  - liability can arise even for potential or imminent harm
  - [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/top-stories/solid-waste-management-rules-2026-supreme-court-directs-centre-to-frame-guidelines-on-environmental-compensation-544325).
- The next hearing was listed for **29 September 2026**. I found no report of its outcome — [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/top-stories/solid-waste-management-rules-2026-supreme-court-directs-centre-to-frame-guidelines-on-environmental-compensation-544325).
- **Date inconsistency:** LiveLaw says the Committee was constituted by an OM dated 9 March 2026 "following an earlier order dated 12 May 2026". The dates are internally inconsistent; the earlier order may be from 2025.
- A Dadra & Nagar Haveli and Daman & Diu PCC circular (updated 30 March 2026) refers to "CPCB Revised Guidelines for Environmental Compensation under Water Act, Air Act and Solid Waste Management Rules" — [Simpliance listing](https://simpliance.in/India/EHS/govt_notification/dadra-and-nagar-haveli/circular-regarding-the-implementation-of-cpcb-revised-guidelines-for-environmental-compensation-under-water-act-air-act-and-solid-waste-management-rules-reg-14158). It is unclear whether this is SWM-2026-specific. It predates the SC's August 2026 direction treating the rule 17(2) guidelines as still to be framed.

**Timelines (Schedule I and dated duties)**
- **Schedule I** sets implementation time from the rules' commencement:
  - million-plus cities: 18 months (about 1 October 2027)
  - 5–10 lakh population: 24 months
  - all urban areas: 36 months
  - rural areas of 20,000+: 18 months
  - rural areas of 10,000–20,000: 24 months
  - rural areas up to 10,000: 36 months
- **Other dated duties:**
  - CPCB portal: within 6 months (rule 16(1))
  - dumpsite mapping: by 31 October 2026 (15(1))
  - grievance mechanism: within 1 year of notification, i.e. about 27 January 2027 (39(35), 40(2)(xv))
  - BWG list: by 1 April each year (39(36))
  - annual returns: by 30 June (6(h), 39(10)–(14))
  - SPCB facility audits: by 30 September (41(5)), published by 31 December (41(6))
  - CPCB annual report: by 30 September (38(1)(vi))

**[2016, SUPERSEDED] comparison:** The 2016 rule for generators read "No waste generator shall throw, burn or burry [sic] the solid waste generated by him, on streets, open public spaces outside his premises or in the drain or water bodies". The 2016 rules also barred sweepers from burning leaves — [NPC toolkit](https://npcindia.gov.in/NPC/Files/delhiOFC/EM/Toolkit-kit-on-Solid-waste-management.pdf); [Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/waste/solid-waste-management-rules-2016-53443). ### Inferences
- **Strongest rule hooks for a Saboot complaint:**
  - rule 5(1)(f) (no throwing, burning or burying)
  - rule 39(7) and 39(15) (ULB must stop burning)
  - rule 39(2)(vii)/(xi) (ULB must plan for garbage vulnerable points and open-dumping plots)
  - rule 15(1) (map dumpsites and GVPs by 31 October 2026)
  - rule 41(4) (SPCB must impose EC "including for indiscriminate dumping of solid waste by local authorities")
  - rule 17(3) (CPCB directs the SPCB if it does not act "in reasonable time")
  - rule 37(ii) (Collector reviews the ULB quarterly)
- **Timing for the pitch:** The 31 October 2026 mapping deadline falls three weeks after the hackathon. Citizen-pinned, satellite-verified sites are directly useful input to the ULB's statutory GVP and dumpsite map.
- **Why two addressees:** Rule 17(3) names the SPCB as the EC-levying authority, and rule 17(3)'s escalation clause names CPCB. A complaint should therefore go to the SPCB, not only the ULB, with the CPCB escalation route noted.
- **Don't promise rupee amounts:** Until the rule 17(2) EC guidelines are notified, the app should not state a specific EC amount under the 2026 Rules. The NGT's 2016 per-incident amounts for open burning (Q4/Q5) can be cited as tribunal-set benchmarks, labelled as NGT orders.

## 3. Who is responsible and where a citizen should complain in Jodhpur; channels and response timelines

### Takeaway
Primary responsibility for dumping and burning in city limits lies with the **Jodhpur Municipal Corporation**, now a single merged body (see the Inferences below for which officers to address under rules 39(12), 39(35) and 39(7)). Enforcement and EC lie with the **RSPCB**, through its Regional Office Jodhpur (rules 41(1)(i) and 41(4)). Oversight lies with the **District Collector** (quarterly review under rule 37(ii), plus nuisance powers under BNSS s.152). In villages, the **Gram Panchayat** is responsible (rule 40(3)). The **NGT Central Zone Bench (Bhopal)** is the judicial forum for Rajasthan.

Response timelines:
- **CPGRAMS** has a 21-day ceiling, but it is an administrative norm under a DARPG office memorandum, not a statute.
- **Swachhata app** service levels are administrative (about 12 hours for garbage dumps, per older reports).
- **Rajasthan Sampark** has no statutory deadline found.
- **NGT applications** have a statutory limitation period (6 months under s.14), not a response deadline.

**District Collector / SDM**
- Rule 37 duties are described in Q2.
- Under **BNSS 2023 s.152** (formerly CrPC s.133), a DM, SDM or empowered Executive Magistrate can issue a conditional order to remove a public nuisance from a public place or way. The magistrate may act on a citizen's complaint, a police report, or other information. If the order is ignored, it is made absolute under s.155 — [Lexibal on BNSS s.152](https://lexibal.com/bnss-section-152-nuisance/); [Vakeel360 CrPC-BNSS mapping](https://vakeel360.com/crpc-to-bnss/section-133-conditional-order-for-removal-of-nuisance).

## 5. Laws covering open burning

### Takeaway
Open burning of municipal waste is covered by four layers:
- **SWM Rules 2026** (rules 5(1)(f), 39(7), 39(15), 40(3)(iii)(d), Schedule IV), enforceable through EC (rule 17) and **EP Act s.15** penalties (now monetary, under the Jan Vishwas amendments).
- The **NGT's 2016 Almitra Patel ban**, with per-incident EC.
- The **Air Act 1981**, now with adjudicating officers and monetary penalties.
- Public-nuisance law: **BNS s.270** and **BNSS s.152**.

CAQM/GRAP directions are NCR-specific and do not apply to Jodhpur.

### Cited Findings
- **SWM Rules 2026:** The burning prohibitions in rules 5(1)(f), 39(7), 39(15) and 40(3)(iii)(d), and the Schedule IV dumpsite fire measures, are listed in Q2 — [Gazette text](https://www.mpcb.gov.in/sites/default/files/standing_orders/SWM_2026.pdf).
- **EP Act s.15 after the Jan Vishwas (Amendment of Provisions) Act, 2023:**
  - New sections 15 and 15A–15D replaced the old ss.15–17.
  - Penalties are monetary only, **Rs 10,000 to Rs 15 lakh**, imposed by adjudicating officers, under procedural rules (the Environment Protection (Manner of Holding Inquiry and Imposition of Penalty) Rules, 2024) — [Law.asia](https://law.asia/new-rules-address-supreme-courts-burning-question/).
  - One summary gives a different floor (Rs 1 lakh to Rs 15 lakh), so the figures conflict — [Shankar IAS (secondary)](https://www.shankariasparliament.com/current-affairs/gs-ii/gs-ii-govt-policies-interventions/the-jan-vishwas-amendment-of-provisions-bill-2023).
  - Rule 17(5) of the SWM Rules 2026 expressly preserves s.15 EP Act action — [Gazette text](https://www.mpcb.gov.in/sites/default/files/standing_orders/SWM_2026.pdf).
- **Air Act 1981 (Jan Vishwas amendments):**
  - The Centre can appoint adjudicating officers.
  - Imprisonment for some non-compliance with s.22 and **s.31A directions** is replaced with penalties of about Rs 10,000 to Rs 15 lakh.
  - Appeals go to the NGT after depositing 10% of the penalty.
  - These details are from bill-stage analyses — [CEEW](https://www.ceew.in/blogs/why-jan-vishwas-bill-has-implications-for-how-india-governs-air-quality); [Vidhi Centre for Legal Policy](https://vidhilegalpolicy.in/research/analysis-of-proposed-amendments-to-the-environmental-laws-in-jan-vishwas-amendment-of-provisions-bill-2022/); [PRS](https://prsindia.org/billtrack/the-jan-vishwas-amendment-of-provisions-bill-2022).
- **NGT ban:** Complete ban on open waste burning with Rs 5,000 / Rs 25,000 per-incident EC (OA 199/2014, 22 December 2016) — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/article/news-ians/ngt-orders-enforcement-of-waste-management-rules-bans-open-burning-of-waste-116122301087_1.html).
- **BNS s.270 (public nuisance)** and **BNSS s.152** (magistrate's order to remove a nuisance) — [Sansa Legal](https://www.sansalegal.com/post/how-to-file-a-public-nuisance-complaint-under-bns-and-municipal-laws-in-india); [Lexibal](https://lexibal.com/bnss-section-152-nuisance/).
- **CAQM satellite protocol for crop-residue burning:**
  - CAQM in 2021 directed adoption of a standard **ISRO/IARI satellite-based protocol** for counting crop-residue fire events across Punjab, Haryana, UP, **Rajasthan** and Delhi — [HT via PressReader, 2021](https://www.pressreader.com/india/hindustan-times-delhi/20210823/281651078190133); [CivilsDaily](https://www.civilsdaily.com/news/air-quality-panel-holding-talks-with-punjab-govt-and-isro-over-tracking-of-stubble-fires/).
  - The protocol is still used in CAQM's 2026 reporting — [CAQM press release, February 2026](https://caqm.nic.in/WriteReadData/LINKS/CAQM%20PR%2016_02_202614d2f5cf-92fe-4504-a8f4-468913f7b2dd.pdf).
  - Farm-fire EC is tiered by landholding: Rs 5,000 below 2 acres, Rs 10,000 for 2–5 acres, Rs 30,000 above 5 acres — [PW Only IAS (secondary)](https://pwonlyias.com/current-affairs/stubble-burning-india-caqm-isro-fire-monitoring/).

### Inferences
- **GRAP does not apply to Jodhpur.** CAQM and GRAP cover the NCR and adjoining areas, and Jodhpur is not in the NCR. This is from general knowledge and was not sourced in this session.
- **CAQM's protocol is still a useful precedent.** A statutory body already uses satellite fire detections, verified on the ground, as the trigger for EC. That is the evidentiary model Saboot follows.
- **What a burning complaint should cite:**
  - rule 5(1)(f) and rule 39(7)/(15) of the 2026 Rules
  - the NGT Almitra Patel ban
  - rule 17 / EP Act s.15
  - optionally, BNSS s.152 for a magistrate's order

## 6. Satellite imagery as evidence; Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA) s.63; making it credible

### Takeaway
Indian courts and the NGT regularly **use and order** satellite imagery, for example:
- the Supreme Court's 2018 Aravalli "31 hills vanished" order (Rajasthan)
- Little Rann of Kutch (2023)
- the Ropar sand-mining case OA 553/2024 (ISRO/NRSC images)
- the Kanwar road tree-felling case (Survey of India images)
- the Pen dumpsite case OA 88/2024 (MRSAC time-series)

The **NGT is not bound by formal rules of evidence** (NGT Act s.19(3)). Before civil and criminal courts, however, electronic records need a **BSA s.63(4) certificate**. Its Schedule Part A requires a hash value; Part B requires expert certification. The Supreme Court upheld this in *Pune Bar Association v. UoI* (22 May 2026).

### Cited Findings
**Courts and the NGT using satellite imagery**
- **Supreme Court, Aravalli (October 2018):**
  - Justices Madan Lokur and Deepak Gupta relied on a Central Empowered Committee report finding that **31 of 128 sampled hillocks** in Rajasthan's Aravallis had disappeared. The bench also perused a Forest Survey of India report.
  - The Court ordered illegal mining in 115.34 ha stopped within 48 hours.
  - [WION](https://www.wionews.com/india-news/31-hills-vanish-in-aravalli-supreme-court-expresses-shock-173131); [Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/archive/nation/-humans-flying-away-with-hills-like-hanuman-sc-shocked-at-illegal-mining-in-rajasthan-672512).
  - Another account gives "28 of 138" hills, so the figures conflict.
- **NGT, Little Rann of Kutch (2023):** A Space Applications Centre assessment comparing Google Earth images from 1985 and 2022 found a "significant increase in salt pan activities" — [DTE court digest, 13 April 2023](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/environment/daily-court-digest-major-environment-orders-april-13-2023--88770).
- **NGT OA 553/2024 (Ropar–Hoshiarpur sand mining):**
  - Under an NGT order dated 28 August 2024, CPCB obtained satellite images from ISRO/NRSC.
  - The tribunal reasoned that non-monsoon imagery over 5 years would show the extent of illegal mining — [CPCB report in OA 553/2024](https://www.greentribunal.gov.in/sites/default/files/news_updates/REPORT%20BY%20CPCB%20IN%20OA%20NO.%20553%20of%202024%20NEWS%20ITEM%20TITLED%20FROM%20ROPAR%20TO%20HOSHIARPUR%20VIA%20HP%2030-KM%20DETOUR%20AS%20ILLEGAL%20MINING%20DAMAGES%20BRIDGE_1.pdf).
- **NGT, Kanwar road (UP), 2024:** The NGT directed the Survey of India to file satellite images showing trees felled. It later issued notice to the Surveyor General for non-compliance — [The Week](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/national/2024/07/13/lgd5-green-kanwar-yatra-road.html); [Bar & Bench](https://barandbench.com/amp/story/news/kawar-marg-tree-felling-ngt-imposes-costs-1-surveyor-general-of-india-non-appearance).
- **NGT OA 88/2024 (dumping by Pen Municipal Council, Raigad):** The joint committee engaged the Maharashtra Remote Sensing Applications Centre (MRSAC) to evaluate land-use change from time-series imagery — [Joint Committee report, OA 88/2024](https://www.greentribunal.gov.in/sites/default/files/news_updates/Report%20of%20the%20Joint%20Committee%20in%20OA%20No.88-2024%20(page%20nos.436-553).pdf).
- **Applicants annex imagery:** For example, in a 2024 Punjab dumpsite filing, OA 668/2023 — [Applicant reply, OA 668/2023](https://www.greentribunal.gov.in/sites/default/files/news_updates/Reply%20of%20Applicant%20in%20OA%20No.%20668%20of%202023%20(Parasher%20Dev%20sharma%20Vs.%20State%20of%20Punjab%20&%20Ors.).pdf).
- **Imagery is contestable:**
  - In the 2017 Art of Living (Yamuna) matter, the respondent attacked an expert report for relying on "a single satellite image dated 5 September 2015 ... during the peak monsoon season" — [Art of Living statement (party source)](https://www.srisriravishankar.org/work-entry/no-data-support-ngt/).
  - In a September 2026 Odisha laterite case, the applicant relies on Google Earth historical imagery. The NGT formed a joint committee to verify — [UNI](https://www.uniindia.com/east-india/law-odisha-ngt-khurda-illegal-mining/174318).
- **Satellite fire counts have known limits:**
  - CAQM's chairman said satellite-overpass fire counts are "not the most accurate picture".
  - An ISRO SAC study found peak burning shifted from about 1:30 pm (2020) to about 5 pm (2024), outside polar-orbiter overpass times.
  - Ground verification by states is being added.
  - [CivilsDaily](https://www.civilsdaily.com/news/air-quality-panel-holding-talks-with-punjab-govt-and-isro-over-tracking-of-stubble-fires/); [PW Only IAS](https://pwonlyias.com/current-affairs/stubble-burning-india-caqm-isro-fire-monitoring/).

**NGT evidence rules (NGT Act 2010, s.19)**
- **s.19(1):** The NGT is not bound by the CPC and is guided by natural justice.
- **s.19(3):** "The Tribunal shall also not be bound by the rules of evidence contained in the Indian Evidence Act, 1872."
- [LawX: NGT Act s.19](https://lawx.in/acts/section/120/1076/9279).

**BSA 2023, s.63 (replaces Evidence Act s.65B)**
- **s.63(4)** requires a certificate in the Schedule format to accompany electronic records:
  - **Part A**, by the party or person in charge of the device, includes the **hash value and the hash algorithm used** (for example, MD5 or SHA).
  - **Part B** is an **expert's certificate**.
  - Commentary notes s.63(4) uses "shall".
  - [LiveLaw explainer](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/articles/electronic-evidence-admissibility-section-63-bhartiya-saksha-adhiniyam-2023-261511); [SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/?p=389747).
- ***Pune Bar Association v. Union of India*** (SC, order of 22 May 2026; 2026 LiveLaw (SC) 551; CJI Surya Kant and Justices Joymalya Bagchi and Vipul M. Pancholi):
  - The Court rejected a challenge to s.63(4) and called the hash value "an electronic fingerprint".
  - It held that the Madras HC view (only s.79A IT Act-notified examiners may sign Part B) is not binding precedent. A person with demonstrable expertise in computer science or cyber forensics may sign Part B. The question was left open.
  - [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/top-stories/supreme-court-rejects-challenge-to-s634-bsa-mandating-hash-value-disclosure-for-electronic-evidence-535950).
- **Pre-BSA precedent:** *Arjun Panditrao Khotkar* (SC 2020) held that the s.65B(4) certificate is a condition precedent for secondary electronic evidence. It can be produced at any stage, and courts can summon the person to issue it — [Arjun Panditrao Khotkar judgment](https://digiscr.sci.gov.in/admin/judgement_file/judgement_pdf/2020/volume%207/Part%20I/Arjun%20Panditrao%20Khotkar%20_%20Kailash%20Kushanrao%20Gorantyal%20And%20Ors._1701334263.pdf). The BNSS took effect on 1 July 2024 — [Vakeel360](https://vakeel360.com/crpc-to-bnss/section-133-conditional-order-for-removal-of-nuisance). The BSA is understood to share that date, but that comes from general knowledge and is not separately sourced here.
- **Foreign analogue (persuasive only):** In *Ory v. City of Naperville* (Illinois appellate court), Google Earth images were excluded because the plaintiff did not authenticate how the images were dated or show that the dates were accurate — [HeplerBroom](https://www.heplerbroom.com/blog/illinois-courts-treatment-of-google-earth-images-as-admissible-evidence).

### Inferences
- **Evidence posture by forum:**
  - **Administrative complaints** (ULB, RSPCB, Collector, Sampark, CPGRAMS) have no admissibility bar. Imagery serves to trigger inspection.
  - **NGT:** Imagery is readily considered (s.19(3)). The NGT typically orders a **joint committee** (SPCB, CPCB, district) or ISRO/NRSC/state remote-sensing centre to verify on the ground.
  - **Civil or criminal courts:** A s.63 certificate (Parts A and B) would be needed.
- **Recommended metadata to attach.** Note that Copernicus/ESA and NASA are the original data producers, so the citizen can certify only their own retrieval and processing.
  - **Sentinel-2:**
    - product ID or granule ID
    - MGRS tile
    - sensing date-time in UTC
    - processing level (L1C or L2A)
    - cloud-cover percentage
  - **FIRMS:**
    - satellite or instrument (VIIRS S-NPP / NOAA-20 / NOAA-21, or MODIS)
    - acquisition date and time
    - lat/long
    - confidence
    - FRP
  - **Retrieval record:**
    - the API URL and the retrieval timestamp
    - a **SHA-256 hash** of each downloaded file and of the generated complaint PDF, to fill BSA Schedule Part A
  - **Ground evidence:**
    - geotagged and timestamped citizen photos
    - before/after image pairs from more than one date
- **Phrasing for burn detections:** Treat FIRMS detections as "thermal anomaly consistent with fire", not proof of waste burning. Pair them with a ground photo to avoid the CAQM-style accuracy objections. Avoid monsoon or cloudy imagery, given the Art of Living-style challenge.
- **Resolution:** Sentinel-2's 10 m resolution may not resolve small dumps. Satellite evidence is strongest for large or growing dumps and landfill spread. Resolution figures are from general knowledge, not sourced here.

### Gaps
- No Indian judgment was found that specifically rules on the admissibility of Google Earth or Sentinel imagery under s.65B or s.63.
- No NGT order was found that relied on NASA FIRMS data specifically.
- The exact text of the BSA Schedule (Part A and Part B fields) was not read from the official Gazette.
- Whether NGT practice still requires s.63 certificates despite s.19(3) was not determined. An unverified inference: s.19(3) refers to the 1872 Act, which the BSA replaced, so a General Clauses Act-style substitution argument likely applies.

## 7. Evidence that citizen waste complaints are closed without resolution

### Takeaway
There is **anecdotal and dated** evidence of "resolved" closures without action:
- Gurugram's Swachh Map app (2018) used fake "cleaned" photos.
- Jalandhar's Swachhata app had dump complaints marked resolved and residents uninstalling the app.
- Mumbai complaints were "rejected" over jurisdiction.
- Rajasthan Sampark reports 98% "disposal", with the state itself issuing 2025–26 directives to verify satisfaction.

**No systematic audit, RTI study, or CAG finding quantifying fake closures** of SWM complaints was found. The synopsis claim "complaints are closed without action" is supportable as a documented pattern, but not as a measured rate.

### Cited Findings
- **Gurugram (Hindustan Times, July 2018):**
  - The Swachh Map app "was found to be using fake images to notify users that places marked as dirty have been cleaned". Reporters found the garbage still there.
  - A later batch of 6 locations marked clean were all still dirty.
  - [Hindustan Times via PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/india/hindustan-times-chandigarh/20180714/281629601041926).
- **Jalandhar (Tribune):**
  - Residents uninstalled the Swachhata app because complaints stayed unresolved; 38 complaints were "re-opened".
  - One resident's dump complaint showed as resolved with nothing done.
  - The health officer cited shortages of sanitation staff. He also said "fake complaints" are often received, a claim without figures.
  - [Tribune](https://m.tribuneindia.com/article/swachhata-app-loses-favour-with-jalandhar-residents/714798).
- **Mumbai:** Unresolved Swachhata complaints were "rejected" when the road was not under BMC — [Elets eGov, 2016](https://egov.eletsonline.com/2016/10/swachhata-app-keeps-mumbai-roads-garbage-free/).
- **Design weakness:** The Swachhata app relies on an official-uploaded "Resolved" photo, and the citizen's only check is to reopen — [Vikaspedia](https://en.vikaspedia.in/viewcontent/e-governance/mobile-governance/swachhata-app).
- **Rajasthan Sampark:**
  - More than 98% of about 73 lakh complaints since 2019 were "disposed" — [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/problems-of-people-of-rajasthan-been-halved-says-cm-ashok-gehlot-1150917.html).
  - In February 2025 the Chief Secretary directed feedback collection "to verify whether their grievances were genuinely addressed" and photo uploads of resolved complaints — [SocialNews](https://www.socialnews.xyz/2025/02/20/rajasthan-chief-secretary-calls-for-timely-resolution-of-complaints-on-sampark-portal/).
  - In March 2026, guidelines targeted complainants who "remain dissatisfied" — [Newsgram](https://www.newsgram.com/india/2026/03/19/rajasthan-govt-issues-directives-for-faster-disposal-of-grievances-on-sampark-portal).

- **CPGRAMS:** DARPG's 2024 OM explicitly bars closing grievances as "Does not pertain to this Ministry/Department" — [Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/india/centre-cuts-public-grievances-redressal-time-to-21-days); [PIB document](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2024/dec/doc20241230478501.pdf).

  - Sent to the government on 12 January 2026 and tabled on 19 February 2026.
  - Of 18 test-checked ULBs, only 6 prepared DPRs under ISWM/SBM 1.0 and 11 under SBM 2.0.
  - Audited against the SWM Rules 2016.
  - [CAG report page](https://cag.gov.in/en/audit-report/details/124004).
  - The full report is about 64 MB and was not read.

  - Only **20.69%** of Rs 292.81 crore released to ULBs for SWM under SBM (2015–17) was spent.

  - **55.41% of urban wards** lacked door-to-door collection in 2016–17.
  - [CAG Report No. 2 of 2018, Chapter 4](https://cag.gov.in/uploads/download_audit_report/2018/Chapter_4_Audit_Findings_on_Urban_Local_Bodies_of_Report_No_2_of_2018_-_Local_Bodies_Government_of_Rajasthan.pdf).
- **ICSSR–CAG colloquium (Jaipur, 13 July 2026):** It recommended digitisation of waste collection and "enhanced monitoring and accountability" — [ICSSR](https://icssr.org/event/cag-icssr-colloquium-state-finances-rajasthan-and-solid-waste-management-urban-local-bodies).
- **Municipal staff burning waste:** A news clipping reports Jaipur's civic body itself burning garbage to keep the city "clean" (date not verified) — [India Environment Portal clipping](https://indiaenvironmentportal.org.in/news-clippings/foul-play-civic-body-burns-garbage-for-a-clean-jaipur).
- **New mechanism still pending:** The SWM Rules 2026 make an online SWM grievance mechanism mandatory only within one year of notification (rule 39(35)). As of October 2026, ULBs need not yet have one — [Gazette text](https://www.mpcb.gov.in/sites/default/files/standing_orders/SWM_2026.pdf).

## 8. Numbers: waste, legacy waste and dumpsites in Rajasthan and Jodhpur

### Takeaway
The latest official state figures found are from Rajasthan's report to the NGT in OA 606/2018 (data to 31 August 2022):
- **6,523 TPD** generated across 196 ULBs
- **3,534 TPD (54%)** processed
- **176 legacy dumpsites** holding **75.4 lakh tonnes**

Jodhpur-specific numbers are old: about 380 TPD in a 2013 study, and about 4 lakh tonnes of legacy waste in a 2022 approval, unit uncertain. No current Keru figures were found.

### Cited Findings
**Rajasthan Local Self Government Department report to the NGT, OA 606/2018 (CPCB format, data "till 31.08.2022")**
- Number of ULBs: 196
- MSW generated: **6,523 TPD**
- Collected: 6,457 TPD (99%)
- Segregated and transported: 4,659 TPD (70%)
- Processed: **3,534 TPD (54.18%)**
- Disposed in landfill: 2,989 TPD (45.72%)
- Door-to-door collection in 110 ULBs, with 86 targeted
- **176 legacy dumpsites** holding **75.4 lakh tonnes**
- About Rs 370 crore needed for remediation
- 24 AMRUT towns with about 45.5 lakh tonnes put to tender
- 15 dumpsites cleared; biomining begun at 21

- Waste-to-energy work orders awarded for **Jaipur and Jodhpur** (2 plants, 1,000 TPD total)

- [Rajasthan report in OA 606/2018](https://www.greentribunal.gov.in/sites/default/files/news_updates/REPORT%20BY%20GOVT.%20OF%20RAJASTHAN%20IN%20OA%20NO.%20606%20of%202018%20(COMPLIANCE%20OF%20MSW%20MANAGEMENT%20RULES,%202016%20FOR%20STATE%20OF%20RAJASTHAN)%201.pdf); [India Environment Portal listing (14 September 2022)](https://indiaenvironmentportal.org.in/in-court/report-by-the-government-of-rajasthan-regarding-compliance-of-municipal-solid-waste-management-rules-2016-14092022).
